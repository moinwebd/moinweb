Freelance Website Designer Prices: What Does a Business Website Cost?

BY MOINUDDIN UPDATED: OCT 2026 10 MIN READ
FIG 01. — WHAT A FREELANCE WEBSITE DESIGNER COSTS, BY PROJECT TYPE

Freelance website designer prices for small business projects typically range from $200 to $700, depending on the number of pages, custom design, features, and content needs. Larger or ecommerce projects are usually quoted individually.

The answer depends on what you need. A simple website that introduces your business is very different from a custom website with advanced functionality, online payments, booking systems, or multiple service pages.

Understanding freelance website designer prices can help you plan your budget, compare proposals, and choose a solution that supports your business goals without paying for features you do not need.

In this guide, we explain how freelance web designer pricing works, what affects the total website design cost, which additional expenses to consider, and how to budget for a professional small business website.

Whether you are starting a new business, improving an existing website, or comparing freelance designers, this guide will help you make a more informed decision. If you are still choosing who to work with, read our guide to hiring a freelance website designer first.

What Determines Freelance Website Designer Prices?

There is no universal price for hiring a freelance website designer. Each project has different requirements, and designers may use different pricing models depending on the work involved.

Before comparing quotes, understand the main factors that influence the cost.

1. Website Size and Number of Pages

The number of pages is one factor that affects website design cost.

A small business website might need only a homepage, about page, services page, and contact page. A larger website may require separate pages for multiple services, locations, case studies, resources, or products.

However, the number of pages is not the only consideration. A page with a unique layout and complex functionality may require considerably more work than a page using an established design template.

Budget tip: List the pages your business needs before requesting quotes. This gives designers a clearer understanding of the project scope.

2. Custom Design vs. Template-Based Design

Some websites use an existing theme or template as their starting point. Others require a custom design developed around the business's brand, audience, and goals.

A template-based approach may be suitable when you have straightforward requirements and want to launch efficiently. A custom design may be more appropriate when you need a distinctive visual identity, specialized layouts, or a tailored user experience.

Neither approach is automatically better for every business. The right choice depends on your objectives, budget, and required functionality.

3. Website Functionality

Features and integrations can have a significant effect on freelance website designer rates and project costs.

Examples include:

  • Contact and inquiry forms.
  • Appointment booking systems.
  • Online payments and ecommerce.
  • Membership or customer account areas.
  • Search and filtering functionality.
  • Multilingual content.
  • Third-party software integrations.
  • Custom animations or interactive elements.

A basic contact form is different from a booking system that manages appointments, sends notifications, and connects to external software.

Identify essential features early so your designer can estimate the work accurately.

4. Content Writing and Image Preparation

Your website needs clear, useful content to explain your business and help visitors make decisions.

Some clients provide all website copy and images. Others need help with content planning, professional copywriting, image selection, editing, or original graphics.

If content creation is part of your project, clarify whether it is included in the quote or billed separately.

You should also confirm that images, fonts, and other visual assets are properly licensed for your intended use.

5. Designer Experience and Specialization

Freelance website designers have different levels of experience, technical skills, and areas of specialization.

A designer who focuses on simple informational websites may price projects differently from someone who builds complex ecommerce websites or develops custom integrations.

More experience does not automatically guarantee better results, and a lower rate does not necessarily mean lower quality.

Evaluate the designer's relevant portfolio, communication, project process, and ability to meet your requirements alongside the quoted price.

6. Project Timeline and Complexity

A project with a tight deadline may require additional planning or a different working arrangement.

Complex projects can also involve more rounds of feedback, technical testing, coordination, and revisions.

Discuss your preferred launch date with the designer before work begins. Agree on realistic milestones and identify any tasks that depend on your own content, approvals, or account access.

Common Freelance Website Designer Pricing Models

Freelance web designers generally use one or more pricing models. Understanding the differences can help you choose the arrangement that suits your project.

Fixed-Price Website Design

With fixed-price pricing, the designer agrees to complete a defined scope of work for an agreed fee.

This approach can be useful when you already know which pages, features, and deliverables you need.

Advantages:

  • Easier to plan the project budget.
  • Clear expectations about deliverables.
  • A defined scope for evaluating completion.
  • Less uncertainty when requirements remain stable.

Potential drawbacks:

  • Additional features may require a separate quote.
  • Major changes can affect the timeline and total cost.
  • Unclear project requirements can lead to disagreements.

Fixed pricing works best when the scope, revision limits, responsibilities, and payment milestones are documented clearly.

Hourly Website Designer Rates

With hourly pricing, you pay for the time the designer spends on the agreed work.

This model can be useful for ongoing improvements, troubleshooting, smaller changes, consultations, or projects where the requirements may evolve.

Advantages:

  • Flexible when priorities change.
  • Suitable for maintenance and ongoing work.
  • Useful when the total scope is difficult to define in advance.

Potential drawbacks:

  • The final cost may be less predictable.
  • Additional requests can increase the total hours.
  • You may need regular progress reports to monitor the budget.

Ask for an estimated number of hours, an explanation of the work involved, and an agreed process for approving additional time.

Monthly Retainer or Ongoing Support

Some freelancers offer ongoing support for a recurring fee.

Depending on the agreement, this may cover website updates, content changes, technical maintenance, performance monitoring, or a specified amount of design and development work.

Before choosing a retainer, clarify what is included, how much work is available each month, whether unused time carries forward, and how additional requests are billed.

How Much Does a Small Business Website Cost?

The total small business website cost depends on the type of website you need and the work required to deliver it.

Rather than assuming every business needs the same package, consider these general project categories.

Project typeWhat is typically includedTypical range
Basic informational site3-5 pages, contact form, responsive layout, basic on-page SEO$200 - $350
Custom small business site6-10 pages, custom layout, service pages, stronger calls to action$350 - $700
Ecommerce siteProducts, checkout, payments, shipping setupQuoted per project
RedesignNew design, content migration, redirectsDepends on size and scope

Ranges are for typical small business projects and exclude hosting, domain, premium tools, and ongoing maintenance. Larger or more complex projects are quoted individually.

Basic Informational Website

A basic website may be appropriate for a business that wants to introduce its services, provide contact information, and establish an online presence.

Typical requirements may include:

  • A homepage.
  • An about page.
  • A services page.
  • A contact page.
  • Responsive layouts.
  • Basic on-page SEO preparation.

This option may suit consultants, independent professionals, and small service businesses with straightforward needs.

Custom Small Business Website

A more customized website may include additional service pages, project galleries, case studies, custom layouts, stronger conversion paths, or integrations with business tools.

This approach may suit businesses that need a more tailored presentation or have several services and customer groups to address.

Ecommerce Website

An ecommerce project may require product listings, payment processing, shipping configuration, tax settings, inventory management, customer notifications, and order management.

The actual requirements depend on the platform and the complexity of the store.

Remember to account for payment processing charges, platform fees, and other recurring expenses in addition to the initial design work.

Website Redesign

A redesign may involve updating the visual appearance, improving navigation, restructuring content, enhancing mobile usability, or replacing outdated technology.

The cost depends on whether you are refreshing the existing website or rebuilding major parts of it.

A redesign may also require redirect planning, content migration, and technical checks to protect existing URLs and search visibility.

Important: These categories describe project scope, not guaranteed prices. Request a quote based on your business requirements instead of relying on a generic estimate.

1. Domain Registration and Renewal

Your domain is the address customers use to visit your website.

Domain costs depend on the extension, registrar, renewal terms, and whether the name is available at standard registration rates or requires a premium purchase.

Confirm who will register and renew the domain and who will own the account.

2. Web Hosting

Hosting stores your website files and makes the website available online.

The appropriate hosting option depends on your website technology, traffic expectations, performance requirements, and technical needs.

A simple static website may have different hosting requirements from a dynamic website or an ecommerce store.

Ask whether hosting is included in the proposal and what it will cost to continue using it after the initial term.

3. Premium Themes, Plugins, and Software

Some projects use paid themes, plugins, fonts, booking systems, email services, or other third-party tools.

These may involve recurring subscriptions, renewal fees, usage limits, or separate licenses.

Ask which tools are required, whether alternatives are available, and who will pay for renewals.

4. Copywriting and Professional Photography

Professional content and original photography can improve the way your business communicates its services.

However, these tasks may not be included in a website design quote.

If you need help creating content, request a separate breakdown so you can decide what to prioritize.

5. SEO Services

Basic on-page SEO preparation may be included in a website project, but a full SEO campaign usually involves additional work.

This can include keyword research, content planning, technical audits, competitor analysis, link acquisition, and ongoing performance monitoring.

Clarify exactly which SEO tasks are included rather than assuming that website design automatically covers every aspect of SEO.

6. Maintenance, Backups, and Security

After launch, websites may need software updates, backups, security monitoring, bug fixes, and content changes.

The level of maintenance depends on your platform and how the website is managed.

A static website and a website that relies on multiple plugins or dynamic services may require different maintenance arrangements.

7. Payment Processing and Transaction Fees

If your website accepts online payments, your payment provider may charge processing fees or other service charges.

These costs are separate from the design fee and can vary by provider, country, payment method, and transaction type.

8. Future Improvements

Your business may eventually need new service pages, updated branding, additional integrations, or improved functionality.

You do not need to build every possible feature at launch. However, it is helpful to choose an approach that supports reasonable future growth.

How to Budget for Your Website Design Project

A practical budget begins with a clear distinction between essential requirements and optional enhancements.

Step 1: Identify Your Must-Have Features

Write down the pages and functions you need to launch successfully.

For example, a local service business may prioritize service information, a quote request form, customer testimonials, and clear contact details.

An online store may need payment processing, product management, and order notifications from the beginning.

Step 2: Separate Initial and Recurring Expenses

Organize your expected expenses into two groups.

Initial expenses may include:

  • Website planning and design.
  • Development and configuration.
  • Initial content creation.
  • Image preparation.
  • Launch testing and handover.

Recurring expenses may include:

  • Domain renewals.
  • Hosting.
  • Premium software subscriptions.
  • Website maintenance.
  • Ongoing SEO or content services.

This separation helps you understand both the cost of launching the website and the cost of keeping it running.

Step 3: Request Itemized Proposals

Ask designers to explain the deliverables included in their quotes.

Where relevant, request separate estimates for design, development, content, integrations, SEO, and maintenance.

Itemized proposals make it easier to compare offers and identify services you may not need immediately.

Step 4: Keep a Contingency for Changes

Even well-planned projects can reveal new requirements.

Consider reserving some budget for unexpected needs or approved scope changes. The appropriate amount depends on your project's uncertainty and financial situation.

Do not treat a contingency as permission for unlimited changes; agree on how extra work will be approved and priced.

Step 5: Prioritize Business Value

A feature is worth considering when it solves a genuine problem for your customers or helps your business operate more effectively.

Start with the improvements that support your primary objective. Add optional features later if they are not essential to the initial launch.

Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Freelance Website Designer

Before accepting a proposal, ask questions that help you understand the price, deliverables, and working relationship.

Questions About Pricing

  • Is the quote fixed-price or hourly?
  • Which pages and features are included?
  • Are content writing and image preparation included?
  • How are additional revisions or new features charged?
  • Are there any third-party or recurring fees?
  • What payment schedule do you use?

Questions About the Project

  • What information do you need before starting?
  • What is the estimated timeline?
  • How many revision rounds are included?
  • How will I review and approve the work?
  • What happens if the project scope changes?

Questions About Ownership and Support

  • Will I receive the website files and agreed deliverables?
  • Who will own and manage the domain and hosting accounts?
  • Are paid tools or subscriptions required?
  • What support is included after launch?
  • Is ongoing maintenance available?

Is Hiring a Freelance Website Designer Worth It?

Hiring a freelance website designer can be worthwhile when the website supports an important business goal and the project is planned appropriately.

A well-structured website can help visitors understand your services, evaluate your business, and contact you. It can also provide a foundation for content marketing and search engine optimization.

However, a website alone does not guarantee leads, sales, or search rankings. Results depend on many factors, including your offer, content, audience, competition, marketing activities, and ongoing improvements.

Before investing, identify the outcome you want to achieve and discuss how the proposed website will support it.

Frequently Asked Questions About Freelance Website Designer Prices

How much does a freelance website designer charge? ▼

Freelance website designer prices vary according to the project's size, complexity, required features, content needs, designer experience, and location. Request a project-specific quote that clearly explains the deliverables and any additional costs.

What is included in a website design cost? ▼

Depending on the agreement, the cost may include planning, visual design, website development, responsive layouts, basic on-page SEO preparation, testing, and launch support. Hosting, content writing, premium software, and ongoing maintenance may be separate expenses.

Is hourly or fixed-price website design better? ▼

Fixed-price quotes are often useful when the project scope is clearly defined. Hourly pricing may be more suitable for ongoing changes or work with uncertain requirements. Compare the terms and expected deliverables before choosing.

What affects freelance website designer rates? ▼

Rates may reflect experience, specialization, project complexity, technical requirements, communication, and the level of service provided. The total project cost also depends on the amount of work required, not just the hourly rate.

How much should a small business budget for a website? ▼

The appropriate budget depends on the business's goals, required pages, functionality, content, and ongoing needs. Start by listing essential requirements, then request itemized proposals and account for recurring expenses.

Are website hosting and domain registration included in the design fee? ▼

Not always. Some designers include initial setup, while others expect clients to purchase hosting and domain services separately. Confirm who pays for renewals and who owns the relevant accounts.

Does website design include SEO? ▼

Some projects include basic on-page SEO preparation, such as page titles, meta descriptions, heading structure, and image optimization. Keyword research, ongoing content optimization, technical audits, and link-building may require separate services.

Why do website design quotes differ so much? ▼

Quotes may cover different scopes, technologies, levels of customization, content services, functionality, revision limits, and support arrangements. Compare proposals based on the same requirements rather than price alone.

Can I reduce website design costs without sacrificing quality? ▼

Yes. Prioritize essential pages and features, provide organized content and images, use a suitable existing design system when appropriate, and postpone nonessential functionality. Clear requirements and timely feedback can also reduce avoidable rework.

Do I need to pay for website maintenance after launch? ▼

Ongoing costs depend on the website's technology and support needs. Some websites require regular software updates and security monitoring, while others may need less frequent technical maintenance. Discuss your responsibilities and available support options before launch.

How much should a local service business spend on a website? ▼

A straightforward site with a few service pages, a contact form, and a mobile-friendly design typically falls within $200 to $700 with a freelancer. The final cost depends on pages, custom design, and features such as booking. Budget separately for hosting, domain, and maintenance.

Is a cheap website designer worth it? ▼

A low price can be reasonable for a simple project, but check what the quote includes. Look for a clear scope, relevant live projects, revision limits, file ownership, and post-launch support rather than choosing on price alone.

Final Thoughts

Understanding freelance website designer prices helps you make a better decision about your business's online presence.

Focus on the complete project scope, the quality of the proposed work, the designer's communication, and the costs that continue after launch. A clear agreement and a realistic budget can help prevent misunderstandings and make the project easier to manage.

You do not need the most complicated website to get started. You need a website that serves your customers, communicates your value, and supports your business goals.
