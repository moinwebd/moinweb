How to Hire a Freelance Website Designer: A Complete Guide for Small Businesses

BY MOINUDDIN UPDATED: OCT 2026 12 MIN READ
FIG 01. — Hiring a freelance website designer: step-by-step guide

To hire a freelance website designer, start by defining your website goals, then review portfolios of similar projects, compare written quotes by scope (not just price), and agree on ownership, revisions, and support before work begins. This guide walks you through each step: what to look for in a portfolio, which questions to ask, how website design pricing works, and a checklist you can use before you commit.

Whether you are launching a new business, redesigning an outdated website, or expanding your online presence, this guide explains how to hire a freelance website designer with confidence.

Why Hire a Freelance Website Designer?

A freelance website designer can provide a more direct working relationship than a traditional agency. Depending on the designer's experience and services, you may communicate with the person responsible for planning, designing, and implementing your website.

Freelancer vs Agency Comparison

Criteria Freelancer Agency
Communication Direct, with the person doing the work Usually through a project manager
Cost Often lower overhead Often higher, with team costs included
Skills Focused on one or a few areas Broader team (design, SEO, copy)
Flexibility Quick decisions, flexible scope More structured process
Best for Defined projects, small businesses Large or multi-service projects

A Website Designed Around Your Business

Your website should reflect your business identity, services, audience, and goals. A freelance designer can help you create a website that communicates what makes your business different instead of relying entirely on a generic template.

Direct Communication

Working directly with a designer can make it easier to discuss your ideas, review progress, and provide feedback. Clear communication is especially important when your project involves multiple pages, custom features, or frequent content updates.

Flexible Project Scope

Freelancers may offer different types of projects, including new website builds, website redesigns, landing pages, portfolio websites, and ongoing improvements.

The available options depend on the designer's skills and workflow, so confirm which services are included before making a decision.

Support for Small Business Goals

A business website should do more than look professional. It should help visitors understand your services, build trust, and take the next step.

Depending on your needs, a designer can help structure service pages, improve navigation, create clear calls to action, and prepare the website for search engine optimization.

Step 1: Define Your Website Goals Before Hiring

Before you search for a freelance website designer, decide what you want your website to accomplish.

Without clear goals, it can be difficult to compare proposals, estimate the required work, or determine whether the finished website meets your expectations.

Identify Your Main Objective

Start by choosing the most important purpose of your website.

For example:

  • Generate inquiries for your services.
  • Help customers request quotes or consultations.
  • Showcase projects and previous work.
  • Sell products or services online.
  • Establish credibility for a new business.
  • Provide useful information to existing customers.

Your primary goal will influence the website structure, content, design, and functionality.

List the Pages You Need

A typical small business website may include:

  • Home: Introduces your business and highlights your main services.
  • About: Explains your background, expertise, and approach.
  • Services: Describes what you offer and who you help.
  • Projects or Portfolio: Demonstrates relevant experience.
  • Contact: Makes it easy for visitors to reach you.
  • Blog or Resources: Publishes helpful information for your audience.

You may not need every page immediately. Start with the pages that support your business goals and expand when necessary.

Decide Which Features Are Essential

Write down any required functionality, such as a contact form, appointment booking, payment processing, customer testimonials, a blog, or a multilingual interface.

Separate essential features from optional improvements. This will help the designer estimate the project accurately and reduce misunderstandings later.

Step 2: Find Freelance Website Designers in the Right Places

Once you understand your requirements, begin looking for designers whose skills match your project.

You can discover freelancers through professional networks, freelance marketplaces, referrals, search engines, and independent design portfolios.

Search for Relevant Experience

Instead of choosing someone solely because they describe themselves as a website designer, look for experience related to your business or project type.

For example, a local service business may need a designer who understands service pages, inquiry forms, mobile usability, and local SEO fundamentals.

An online store may require experience with product pages, checkout flows, payment integrations, and inventory-related functionality.

Review Their Online Presence

A professional website can help you understand a designer's style, services, communication approach, and previous work.

Look for clear service descriptions, relevant projects, working links, and straightforward contact information.

You can start by exploring the website design portfolio for local service businesses to see examples of website design work.

Compare More Than One Candidate

When possible, shortlist a few designers rather than immediately hiring the first person you find.

Compare their relevant experience, portfolio quality, proposed process, communication, technical capabilities, and overall project fit.

The goal is not necessarily to find the cheapest designer. It is to find someone who can deliver the work your business actually needs.

Step 3: Evaluate the Designer's Portfolio

A portfolio is one of the most useful resources when deciding whether to hire a freelance website designer.

However, attractive screenshots alone do not tell you everything about a website's quality.

Look for Relevant Projects

Review projects that resemble the website you want to build.

Pay attention to:

  • Visual consistency and professional presentation.
  • Clear navigation and logical page structure.
  • Readable typography and effective spacing.
  • Mobile-friendly layouts.
  • Relevant calls to action.
  • Appropriate use of images and content.
  • Ease of finding important information.

If your business relies on generating inquiries, consider whether the examples make it easy for visitors to understand the service and contact the business.

Test Live Websites

If live project links are available, open them on a desktop and a mobile device.

Check whether the navigation works, buttons lead to the correct destinations, forms function properly, and pages remain readable on smaller screens.

You can also use tools such as Google PageSpeed Insights to investigate performance.

Remember that performance can depend on hosting, third-party scripts, content, and other factors—not only the designer's work.

Ask About the Designer's Role

Some portfolio projects involve several people, including developers, copywriters, marketers, and other designers.

Ask which parts the freelancer personally handled. This helps you understand their actual skills and whether they can deliver the work your project requires.

Look for Evidence, Not Just Claims

Testimonials and case studies can provide useful context when they are genuine and relevant.

If a designer claims to have improved traffic, conversions, or sales, ask what was measured and how the results were established. A professional designer should be willing to explain the scope of their contribution without exaggerating outcomes.

From our experience: On one local service project, unclear scope led to three extra revision rounds. Since then, we list every page and feature in the quote before starting.

Step 4: Understand Website Design Pricing

Typical price ranges by project type (prices vary by location and scope; always request a written quote):

Project type Typical scope Estimated range
Simple brochure site 3-5 pages, contact form $500 - $1,200
Local service business site 5-10 pages, service pages, local SEO basics $1,200 - $3,000
E-commerce site Product pages, checkout, payments $2,500 - $6,000+

The cost of hiring a freelance website designer varies considerably. There is no single price that applies to every project.

A simple informational website and a complex website with custom functionality require different amounts of planning, design, development, testing, and maintenance.

Factors That Affect Website Design Costs

Project Size and Number of Pages: A website with a homepage, about page, services page, and contact page generally requires less work than a large website with many unique layouts and content sections. The number of pages matters, but so does the complexity of each page.

Custom Design Requirements: A project that uses an existing design system may require less work than one involving original visual concepts, custom illustrations, detailed interactions, or specialized layouts.

Website Functionality: Features such as booking systems, online payments, membership areas, advanced forms, and third-party integrations can increase project complexity. Discuss these requirements early so the designer can estimate the work accurately.

Content Creation: Some projects include writing website copy, selecting or preparing images, and organizing existing content. Other projects require the client to supply everything. Clarify who is responsible for content creation and whether revisions are included.

SEO Requirements: Basic on-page SEO preparation may include page titles, meta descriptions, heading structure, descriptive URLs, image alt text, and internal links. More extensive SEO work—such as keyword research, content strategy, technical audits, and ongoing optimization—may require a separate scope of work.

Maintenance and Ongoing Support: After launch, your website may need updates, backups, security monitoring, content changes, performance checks, or technical support. Ask whether maintenance is included in the project or offered as a separate service.

Ask for a Clear Quote

A useful proposal should explain what you will receive, what is excluded, how revisions work, and what additional services may cost.

Before accepting a quote, confirm:

  • The number and type of pages.
  • Included design and development work.
  • Required functionality and integrations.
  • Content and image responsibilities.
  • Revision limits and approval stages.
  • Estimated delivery schedule.
  • Payment milestones.
  • Hosting, domain, and third-party costs.
  • Post-launch support and maintenance.

Compare proposals based on their scope and value rather than the total price alone.

Step 5: Ask These Questions Before Hiring

A short conversation before committing to a project can reveal whether a designer is a good fit for your business.

Questions About Experience

  1. Have you worked on websites similar to mine?
  2. Can I review relevant live projects in your portfolio?
  3. Which parts of those projects did you personally handle?
  4. What platform or technology would you recommend for my requirements, and why?

Questions About Process

  1. What information do you need from me before starting?
  2. What are the main stages of your design process?
  3. How often will I receive updates?
  4. How will I review the design and request revisions?

Questions About Pricing and Ownership

  1. What exactly is included in your quote?
  2. How are additional features or changes priced?
  3. Who owns the website files, content, and design deliverables after payment?
  4. Will I have access to the website's administration and hosting accounts?

Questions About Launch and Support

  1. Will you test the website on mobile devices and different browsers?
  2. What happens if something does not work after launch?
  3. Is ongoing maintenance available?
  4. Will you provide instructions for making basic content updates?

You do not need to ask every question in a single conversation. Focus on the ones that matter most to your project and request written clarification for important agreements.

Step 6: Check Communication and Project Management

A designer's technical ability is important, but communication can also have a major impact on your experience.

Before hiring, pay attention to how the designer handles your initial questions.

Look for Clear Explanations: A good working relationship depends on understanding the project, its limitations, and the decisions being made. Your designer should be able to explain technical recommendations in language you understand and ask relevant questions about your business.

Agree on Communication Methods: Decide whether you will communicate through email, video calls, a project management tool, or another agreed channel. Also establish a reasonable response schedule, progress update frequency, and approval process.

Agree on a Realistic Timeline: The delivery timeline depends on the project's size, functionality, content readiness, and feedback process. Ask for an estimated schedule with milestones, such as discovery, design approval, development, testing, and launch. Remember that delays can occur if content, credentials, or approvals are not provided on time. Make sure both parties understand their responsibilities.

Step 7: Understand the Contract and Payment Terms

Before work begins, document the agreement in writing.

A clear contract helps both parties understand the expected deliverables and provides a reference if questions arise during the project.

What Should a Website Design Agreement Include?

At a minimum, consider documenting:

  • Project scope and deliverables.
  • Included pages and features.
  • Design revisions and change requests.
  • Project milestones and estimated timeline.
  • Fees and payment schedule.
  • Client responsibilities.
  • Ownership and licensing of deliverables.
  • Confidentiality and access to business information.
  • Cancellation and dispute procedures.
  • Post-launch support and warranty terms, if offered.

The appropriate terms depend on your location, the designer's location, and the nature of the project. For important legal questions, consider advice from a qualified professional.

Use a Clear Payment Structure: Freelancers may use deposits, milestone payments, or other arrangements depending on the project. Understand when payments are due, which milestones trigger payment, and what happens if the project scope changes. Avoid relying entirely on informal messages when important financial or ownership terms are involved.

Step 8: Confirm Website Ownership and Access

Your business should understand how it will access and manage its website after launch.

This is especially important if you plan to work with different designers or developers in the future.

Ask About Domain and Hosting: Clarify who will register and renew the domain, manage hosting, and pay any recurring service fees. Where practical, keep ownership and billing of essential business accounts under your control. Grant the designer the access needed to complete the work.

Confirm Access to Website Files: Depending on the technology used, your handover may include source files, website administration access, configuration details, and documentation. For a static HTML/CSS/JS website, clarify how you will receive the HTML, CSS, JavaScript, images, and other required assets.

Understand Third-Party Licenses: Some websites rely on paid themes, fonts, plugins, stock images, or external services. Ask which items require ongoing subscriptions and whether their licenses can be transferred or continued under your ownership.

Step 9: Make Sure SEO and Mobile Usability Are Considered

A beautiful website may still struggle to attract and serve visitors if its content is poorly organized, its pages are difficult to use, or search engines cannot access important content.

You do not necessarily need a comprehensive SEO campaign as part of every design project. However, basic technical and on-page considerations should be discussed.

Essential SEO Considerations

Ask whether the project includes:

  • Unique page titles and meta descriptions.
  • Clear heading structure.
  • Descriptive URLs.
  • Relevant internal links.
  • Appropriate image optimization and alt text.
  • An XML sitemap where appropriate.
  • Indexability and crawlability checks.
  • Redirects for changed URLs, when necessary.
  • Google Search Console setup or verification, if agreed.

SEO results are not guaranteed by a website redesign. Search visibility also depends on competition, content quality, relevance, links, technical conditions, and ongoing work.

Essential Mobile and Performance Checks: Before launch, verify that the website works on common screen sizes, the text remains readable, buttons are easy to use, and navigation functions properly. Test contact forms, links, and other important features. Review page performance and fix significant issues where practical. Accessibility should also be considered, including keyboard navigation, form labels, image descriptions, and sufficient color contrast.

Step 10: Prepare for a Smooth Website Launch

Your involvement does not end when you hire a designer. Providing the right information and reviewing progress promptly can help the project move forward.

Prepare Your Business Materials

Gather the information your designer will need, including:

  • Business name and contact details.
  • Logo and brand guidelines, if available.
  • Descriptions of your products or services.
  • Existing website content.
  • Relevant project images.
  • Customer testimonials you have permission to publish.
  • Preferred contact methods.
  • Domain and hosting information, when needed.

Do not send passwords through insecure channels. Use appropriate account invitations or secure access-sharing methods whenever possible.

Review the Website Before Launch

Use a launch checklist to confirm that:

  • All agreed pages are present.
  • Content is accurate and proofread.
  • Navigation and buttons work correctly.
  • Forms submit successfully.
  • The layout works on mobile devices.
  • Images display properly.
  • SEO metadata is in place where required.
  • Important pages are accessible to search engines.
  • Privacy notices and other required disclosures are addressed.
  • Backup and handover arrangements are clear.

Once the website is live, monitor it and resolve any launch-related issues covered by your agreement.

Red Flags vs Good Signs When Hiring

Avoiding a few common mistakes can save time, reduce confusion, and help you make a more informed decision.

🚩 Red flags ✅ Good signs
Guarantees #1 Google ranking Explains realistic goals and what affects results
No live portfolio links Shows relevant, working live projects
Vague quote with no scope Itemized quote with inclusions and exclusions
Pressure to pay everything upfront Milestone-based payments
Won’t give you ownership of files Clear ownership and handover in writing
Slow or unclear replies Prompt, clear communication from the first message

Practical Checklist: Before You Hire a Freelance Website Designer

Use this checklist to compare potential designers and prepare your project.

  • I have defined the main goal of my website.
  • I have listed the pages and essential features I need.
  • I have reviewed relevant portfolio examples.
  • I have checked live websites on desktop and mobile.
  • I understand the designer's role and experience.
  • I have discussed pricing and project scope.
  • I know how revisions and additional requests are handled.
  • I have agreed on communication methods and milestones.
  • I understand website ownership and account access.
  • I have discussed SEO, performance, and accessibility requirements.
  • I have reviewed the contract and payment terms.
  • I understand the handover and maintenance arrangements.

If several of these items remain unclear, ask for clarification before committing to the project.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I hire a freelance website designer? ▼

Start by defining your website goals, required pages, functionality, and budget. Research designers, review relevant portfolios, discuss the project scope, compare proposals, and agree on pricing, deadlines, ownership, and support in writing before work begins.

How much does it cost to hire a freelance website designer? ▼

The cost depends on the number of pages, design complexity, functionality, content requirements, integrations, and support arrangements. Request a detailed quote based on your actual requirements rather than relying on a generic price estimate.

What should I look for in a freelance website designer? ▼

Look for relevant portfolio examples, clear communication, appropriate technical skills, a transparent project process, realistic expectations, and written terms covering deliverables, payments, ownership, and maintenance.

Should I hire a freelance website designer or a web design agency? ▼

A freelancer may be a good fit when you want direct communication and a clearly defined project. An agency may be more suitable when you need a larger team or several specialized services. The right choice depends on your project's complexity, support requirements, budget, and preferred working relationship.

Can a freelance website designer help with SEO? ▼

Some designers provide basic on-page SEO and technical preparation, while others specialize primarily in visual design or development. Ask whether keyword research, content optimization, technical SEO, and ongoing search optimization are included or offered separately.

How long does it take to build a business website? ▼

The timeline depends on the number of pages, custom functionality, design process, content readiness, and approval schedule. Ask the designer for a project-specific estimate with milestones rather than assuming every website takes the same amount of time.

Will I own my website after the project is complete? ▼

Ownership depends on the contract, applicable law, third-party licenses, and the deliverables agreed upon. Confirm who owns the website files, design assets, content, domain, and related accounts before the project begins.

What information should I give a website designer? ▼

Provide your business goals, target audience, service details, preferred pages, brand assets, existing content, examples of websites you like, and any required features. Clear information helps the designer plan a more appropriate solution.

Do I need a new website or just a redesign? ▼

If your existing website still meets your needs, targeted improvements may be enough. A redesign may be worth considering when the current website has persistent usability problems, outdated functionality, poor mobile presentation, or a structure that no longer supports your business goals.

What happens after my website launches? ▼

Depending on your agreement, post-launch work may include bug fixes, content updates, backups, security maintenance, performance monitoring, and SEO improvements. Confirm what support is included and what requires a separate payment.
